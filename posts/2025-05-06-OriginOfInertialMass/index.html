What is the Origin of Inertial Mass?

We propose that the origin of inertial mass is found in the Principle of Conservation of Energy and Weber electrodynamics. Let \(H\) be Weber’s electrodynamic Hamiltonian of an \(N\)-body system which is otherwise isolated from the environment. We imagine \(N \to +\infty\). For a given system we define the inertial mass operator \(A\) as the unique minimal energy symmetric operator satisfying \(A v = \nabla_v H\). Here \(v\) denotes the velocities of the particles relative to the centre of mass. The eigenvalues of the symmetric operator \(A\) correspond to the classical scalar masses of the particles in the system. In this viewpoint, inertial mass is a “proportionality constant” between velocities and \(\nabla_v H\). If \(H\) is the Hamiltonian for the total electrodynamic system, then we find \(A=\nabla^2_{vv}H\) is a symmetric operator satisfying the key equation and also minimal energy. This is a convenient representation formula for \(A\).

The basis of Weber’s law assumes the existence of a well-defined reduced mass \(\mu_{ij}\) for every two-body subsystem of particles \(p_i, p_j\). The reduced mass \(\mu_{ij}\)is defined asymptotically as the inertial mass when \(p_i, p_j\) are infinitely far apart, i.e. \(r_{ij}^{-1} \approx 0\).

Our definition of effective inertial mass requires the nonvanishing \(\nabla_v H \neq 0\). This is important hypothesis. Our construction implies that effective inertial mass is only defined with respect to particles which have some nontrivial amount of radial kinetic energy. In particular a two-body system with zero relative velocity \(\texttt{rdot}=0\) will not have a well-defined inertial mass. Thus inertial mass is strictly defined for objects with relational energy. This is related to Weber’s idea that all motion is a composition of radial and nonradial motions. However only radial forces belong to the system itself, and the nonradial forces are effects of the environment outside the system.

Our method of defining effective inertial mass basically via the equation \(A v = \nabla_v H\) and \(A=\nabla^2_{vv}H\) is only valid when \(\nabla_v H\) is nonzero, and this essentially occurs only if the radial projection \(\dot{r} = \hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \Delta v\) is nonzero. This requires the existence of some relative energy. Therefore the effective inertial mass of systems which have zero relative kinetic energy cannot be known nor defined meaningfully.

Here is a classical example which contains the germ of the idea. Classically inertial mass is observed through billiards and calculated from the Newtonian principle of conservation of linear momentum. In a purely elastic collision, inertial mass is deduced from action-reaction \(m_1 v_1 = - m_2 v_2\) for any two objects with masses \(m_1, m_2\) and velocities \(v_1, v_2\). Suppose we have a reference mass \(m_1\) with velocity \(v_1\) which impacts a second body. If we measure the reaction velocity \(v_2\) of the second body, then we deduce the second mass \(m_2\) according to the Conservation of Linear Momentum. But if the initial body has zero velocity, then the reactant velocity \(v_2\) is also zero and we cannot deduce the second mass. In this case the Conservation equation becomes trivial \(0=0\). This is equivalent to \(\nabla_v H = 0\).

Thus we validate Mach’s idea (Mach 1893) that “the true definition of mass can be deduced only from the dynamical relations of bodies”. Mach sought the origin of inertial mass in the stars at infinity, and predicted that the distribution of matter in the universe is what determines the inertial mass of an object. Thus Mach argued “if there were only one object in an otherwise empty universe, it would have no inertia.”

Newton’s Vis Insita

What is mass?

Intuitively we think of “mass” as representing the quantity of matter present in a object. This was Newton’s original idea. Newton (Newton 1687) defined “innate mass” (vis insita) as measuring the quantity of matter “arising from it’s density and bulk conjointly”. But Newton defined vis insita dynamically as “[that] power of resisting by which every body, as much as in it lies, endeavours to perservere in its present state, whether it be of rest, or of moving uniformly forward in a right line.” “This force is ever proportional to the body whose force it is; nor does it differ in any thing from the inactivity of the matter, but in our manner of conceiving it. A body, upon account of the inactivity of matter, is not without difficulty put out of its state of rest or motion. Upon which account, this vis insita may, by a most significant name, be called inertia (inactivity)”.

Thus Newton apparently defined vis insita as an internal property of matter, the sum of all atomic masses constituting a material body. See Newton’s use of terms “as much as in it lies” and “the inactivity of matter”. Ernst Mach famously critiqued Newton’s mechanics in his Science of Mechanics (Mach 1893). Mach introduced the idea that vis insita and inertial mass was an external property, depending on the interactions of matter with all the stars, universe, and environment at infinity.

Mach’s Critique of Newton

Newton’s definition of “mass” was famously criticized by Ernst Mach as circular, since density as “mass per unit matter” is still undefined. Mach wrote “[Newton’s] Definition I is, as has already been set forth, a pseudo-definition. The concept of mass is not made clearer by describing mass as the product of the volume into the density, as density itself denotes simply the mass of unit volume. The true definition of mass can be deduced only from the dynamical relations of bodies.”

Our goal is to demonstrate how mass can deduced from Weber’s electrodynamic relations and the Principle of Dynamic Equilibrium. This is similar to AKT Assis’ argument (Assis 1999) except Prof Assis restricts himself to a gravitational form of Weber’s law, and this law is speculative. Our contribution is to derive inertial mass strictly from electrodynamic hypotheses.

Liebniz’ Vis Viva

In classical mechanics the kinetic energy (Liebniz’ vis viva) is defined in an absolute reference frame as \(T=\frac{1}{2} mv^2\). The gradient satisfies \(\nabla_v T = m v\) and obviously \(\nabla^2_{vv}T=m\). We interpret these calculations as a type of definition of inertial mass. However this does not explain the origin of mass, nor does it predict the variable effective inertial mass as arising from Weber electrodynamics. Furthermore Newton argues that vis insita is internal resistance to change in motion, while Liebniz’ vis viva is a measure of power caused by the object’s motion. Our proposal is that vis viva and vis insita are both caused by resistance of the environment to change in the object’s motion. All forces are action-reactions satisfying Conservation of Energy.

\(\texttt{WeberDrift}\)

In Newtonian mechanics, the idea of drift is contained in Newton’s Principle of Inertia (so-called First Law):

  • “Lex I (First Law): Corpus omne perseverare in statu suo quiescendi vel movendi uniformiter in directum, nisi quatenus a viribus impressis cogitur statum illum mutare.”
  • Every body continues in its state of rest, or of uniform motion in a straight line, unless it is compelled to change that state by forces impressed upon it.

We observe that inertial mass is not defined by this principle. In the classical case, drift represents constant momentum \(p=mv\), with \(m\) indeterminate.

Weber electrodynamics does not directly have a definition of “momentum” analogous to Newton. Rather, we define Weber Drift as the equations of motion corresponding to the Weberian relational kinetic energy \(H_T=T\).

In Newton’s formulation, the Hamiltonian \(H_T\) defines a system in the “absence of external forces” which evolves according to kinetic energy only. In classical case this is represented by \(p'=0\) hence constant classical momentum \(p=mv\). This is the “momentum drift” of the system. In the classical drift, we see KE is preserved. In the Weber Drift we see the motion is not uniform linear.

Mass Defects

Weber’s Electrodynamic Derivation of \(E=\mu c^2\)

We argue that \(E=m c^2\) is the most famous and the least understood equation of physics. Our position, based on Weber electrodynamics, is that \(E=\mu c^2\) is an electrodynamic expression of the energy required to push two electrons \((-1)\) and \((-1)\) to within the Weber critical radius to fuse and form the stable \((-2)\) electric molecule. Thus \(E=\mu c^2\) represents the energy required to fuse \[(-1) + (-1) \to (-2).\]

Einstein’s mass-energy equivalence hypothesizes that \(E=mc^2\) is a universal physical law. See Einstein’s 1905 article (Einstein 1905).

“Die Masse eines Körpers ist ein Maß für seinen Energieinhalt; ändert sich jener um \(L\), so ändert sich dessen Masse um \(L/c^2\).” That is, “The mass of a body is a measure of its energy content. If that energy changes by \(L\) so changes its mass by $L/c^2.”

Our critique of Einstein’s universal law presents many alternative explanations to keystone physics experiments, especially related to mass defects and energy radiation. For example, the standard model of nuclear fusion is based on the idea of four hydrogen nuclei (or deuterium or tritium) fusing to form a Helium atom. But there is a mass defect of four hydrogen nuclei being slightly greater than the mass of Helium. Einstein’s general energy formula predicts the mass defect is represented by energy radiation. Sir. A. Eddington used this line of reasoning to predict that hydrogen to helium fusion is the cause of the Sun’s radiation. We do not necessarily agree. This is subject of further articles.
