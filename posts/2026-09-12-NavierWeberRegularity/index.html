Regularity of Global Solutions of Incompressible Navier-Weber Equations

We provide a proof of Global Regularity of solutions to Incompressible Navier-Weber equations. The results are obtained by applying the Beale-Kato-Majda Integral Criterion, and directly estimating the integral using standard Holder, Sobolev, Gagliardi-Nirenberg, and Calderon-Zygmund inequalities. The estimates were generated by personal Gemini notebook prompts.

The question is: How to prove the global smoothness of Navier-Weber solutions \(u(x,t)\) assuming smooth initial conditions \(u(x,0)\)?

We recall Navier-Weber equations are a conservative Hamiltonian system. The experts have standard lines of attack for this question. We outline the standard arguments below. Here we admit the estimates depend on detailed Gemini notebook prompts.

We emphasize that our discussion applies to \(\mu_0=0\) zero classical viscosity, although the estimates continue to be valid if we assume \(\mu_0 \neq 0\).

Standard Regularity Theory

The \(3D\) incompressible Navier-Weber fluids equations are defined by \[\rho \left( \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t} + u_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} \right) = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i} + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j} \left( \mathbb{T}_{ij}^{\text{non-linear}} + \mathbb{V}_{ij}^{\text{viscoelastic}} \right).\]

Here we have \(\mathbb{T}^{\text{non-linear}} \propto \nu_{\text{non}} (\nabla u)^3\) and \(\mathbb{V}^{\text{viscoelastic}} \propto \eta_v \dot{V}\).

In our derivations we assume the fluid has zero linear viscosity, i.e. we assume \(\mu_0 \Delta=0\) vanishes identically. This means our arguments are slightly modified from the usual Navier-Stokes approach. Indeed this is fundamental to our hypothesis that fluids are conservative, not dissipative.

BKM Regularity Criterion

The standard regularity theory depends on the Beale-Kato-Majda (BKM) Criterion. The following proposition is surely well-known to experts:

Proposition: Local smooth solutions \(u\in H^s\) extend unique to global smooth solutions for any \(T>0\) if and only if the following integral converges for all \(T\):

\[\int_0^T \Phi(t)dt = \int_0^T (||\nabla u(t)||_{L^\infty} + ||\nabla u||^2_{L^\infty} + \frac{\eta_v}{\rho}||\nabla \dot{V}||_{L^\infty} < +\infty. \]

One finds Gronwall’s inequality has the form \[\mathscr{E}_s(T) \leq \mathscr{E}_s(0) \exp(C \int_0^T \Phi(t) dt).\] If the integral \(\int \Phi\) converges, then the Sobolev energy \(\mathscr{E}_s(T)\) is finite, and consequently \(|u(T)|_{H^s} \leq \sqrt{2 \mathscr{E}_s(T)} < +\infty\). This implies no finite-time blow-up occurs.

In the standard arguments, everything depends on proving finiteness of the BKM integral \(\int \Phi\). We break this integral into three parts \(A+B+C\).

Proof 1: Regularity for Small Initial Data

For small initial values \(\| \nabla u\|^{H^s} \leq \delta\), the usual arguments establish $ _s(t) _s(0) e^{-t}$ for all time. The Sobolev embeddings \(H^{s-1} \hookrightarrow L^\infty\) implies \(|| \nabla u (t)||_{L^\infty} \leq C e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}t}\) for all \(t\). This means the BKM integral converges for all time. This establishes:

Proposition: Navier-Weber solutions are globally smooth for small initial data.

Proof 2: Regularity for Large Initial Data

There is another argument suggested by Gemini and probably well known to experts, which provides a priori bounds on all the quantities in the BKM integral. The argument depends on establishing three key estimates, namely the finiteness of the three integrands in the BKM integral.

Estimate A

The first estimate is:

Proposition: If \(\nu_{non} >0\), then the integral \[\int_0^T ||\nabla u(t)||_{L^\infty} dt \leq \frac{C}{\nu_{\text{non}}^{1/8}} \left( \int_0^T \mathscr{D}_2(u(t))dt \right)^{1/8} \left(\int_0^T \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^{6/7} dt \right)^{7/8} <+\infty\] is finite for all time \(T\).

The proof is an exercise in various inequalities, namely Agmon inequality, Holder Inequalities, Gagliardo-Nirenberg Interpolations, and Cauchy-Schwartz inequality. Admittedly we did not discover this argument, but learned it from the literature.

Estimate B

We estimate the second term in the BKM Criterion.

Lemma: Sobolev inequalities imply \[\mathbf{\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^\infty}^2 \le C_{\text{Agmon}}^2 \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2} \|D^3 u(t)\|_{L^2}}\]

Define the dissipation functional \(\mathscr{D}_2(u)\) by the formula \[\mathscr{D}_2(u) = \nu_{\text{non}} \int |\nabla u(x,t)|^2 \left| D^{3} u(x,t) \right|^2 dx.\] Obviously \(\mathscr{D}_2 \geq 0\).

Holder’s Inequality implies \[\|D^3 u(t)\|_{L^2}^2 = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} |D^3 u|^2 dx \leq \left( \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} |\nabla u|^2 |D^3 u|^2 dx \right)^{1/2} \left( \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} \frac{|D^3 u|^2}{|\nabla u|^2} dx \right)^{1/2}.\]

Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequalities imply:

\[\|D^3 u(t)\|_{L^2} \leq \frac{C}{\nu_{\text{non}}^{1/4}} \left( \mathscr{D}_2(u(t)) \right)^{1/4} \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^{1/2}.\]

Combining this expression with the above square Sobolev estimate, we obtain the inequality:

\[\mathbf{\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^\infty}^2 \le \frac{C_{\text{interp}}^2}{\nu_{\text{non}}^{1/4}} \left( \mathcal{D}_2(u(t)) \right)^{1/4} \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^3)}^{3/2}}.\]

Integrating the above estimates and applying Holder inequality with conjugate exponents \(p=4\), \(q=4/3\) yields:

\[\int_0^T \left( \mathscr{D}_2(u(t)) \right)^{1/4} \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^{3/2} dt \le \mathbf{\left( \int_0^T \mathscr{D}_2(u(t)) \, dt \right)^{1/4} \left( \int_0^T \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2 \, dt \right)^{3/4}}.\]

Now we have \[\int_0^T \mathcal{D}_2(u(t)) \, dt \le \mathcal{E}_2(0) < \infty.\]

Moreover we have \[\int_0^T \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2 \, dt \le T \sup_{t \in [0, T]} \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^2}^2 \le C(\eta_v, \rho) \, T \, \mathcal{E}_2(0) < \infty.\]

Substituting these estimates into the Holder estimate yields:

\[\int_0^T \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^\infty}^2 dt \le \frac{C_{\text{interp}}^2}{\nu_{\text{non}}^{1/4}} \left( \mathcal{E}_2(0) \right)^{1/4} \left( C(\eta_v, \rho) \, T \, \mathcal{E}_2(0) \right)^{3/4}\] which is equivalent to \[\int_0^T \|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^\infty}^2 dt \leq \frac{C}{\nu_{non}^{1/4}} T^{3/4} {\mathscr{E}}_2(0) < +\infty.\]

Estimate C

The third estimate controls the viscoelastic term \(||\nabla \dot{V}||_{L^\infty}\). Again the arguments were provided by Gemini.

Recall \(\dot{V} = \frac{D}{Dt}(\nabla u) = \frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla u) + u \cdot \nabla(\nabla u)\).

The first observation is to rewrite the Navier-Weber equations in terms of a so-called spatial Helmholtz operator:

\[(Id - \frac{\eta_v}{\rho} \nabla^2) \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t} = F_i(u, \nabla u, \nabla^2 u)\] where the RHS is defined as:

\[F_i := -u_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} - \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i} + \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial \mathbb{T}_{ij}^{\text{non-linear}}}{\partial x_j} + \frac{\eta_v}{\rho} \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j} \left( u_k \frac{\partial^2 u_i}{\partial x_k \partial x_j} \right)\]

Lemma: Positivity \(\lambda:=\eta_v/\rho > 0\) implies the operator \(Id - \frac{\eta_v}{\rho}\nabla^2\) is a positive definite strongly elliptic linear operator on \({\mathbf{R}}^3\).

Lemma: The operator \(Id - \frac{\eta_v}{\rho}\nabla^2\) is inverted by convolution with Yukawa’s Green’s function \[G_\lambda(x):=\frac{1}{4 \pi \lambda |x|} \exp (-|x|/\sqrt{\lambda}).\]G

The above lemma implies \(\frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t}(x,t)=(Id - \lambda \nabla^2)^{-1} F_i = \int G_\lambda(x-y) F_i(y)dy\).

The standard Calderon-Zygmund elliptic regularity theory implies that inverting the Helmholtz operator improves solutions by two full spatial derivatives, namely: \((Id-\lambda \nabla^2)^{-1}\) defines an operator from \(L^p\) to \(W^{2,p}\), and we obtain \[\left\| \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(t) \right\|_{W^{2,p}} \leq C_{\eta_v, \rho} \|\mathbf{F}(t)\|_{L^p}.\]

The definition of \(W^{2,p}\) implies \[||\nabla \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}||_{L^\infty} \leq C |F(u, \nabla u, \nabla^2 u)|_{L^\infty}.\]

The definition of \(\dot{V}\) implies \[\nabla \dot{V} = \nabla (\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla u) + u \cdot \nabla(\nabla u)).\]

Now we use Sobolev embedding inequalities for \(s > 3/2\) to estimate \(|\nabla \dot{V}|_{L^\infty}\), namely:

\[|\nabla \dot{V}|_{L^\infty} \leq C_{\eta_v, \rho, \nu_{non}} (\mathscr{E}_s(t) + \mathscr{E}_s(t)^{3/2}).\]

However if we substitute the energy inequality \[\mathscr{E}_s(t) \leq \mathscr{E}_s(0) e^{CK} < +\infty\] then we obtain the finiteness of the integral for all \(T\):

Proposition: The gradient of the viscoelastic term \(\nabla \dot{V}\) is integrable and satisfies \[\int_0^T ||\nabla \dot{V}(t)||_{L^\infty} dt \leq C \int_0^T ((\mathscr{E}_s(t) + \mathscr{E}_s(t)^{3/2})dt < +\infty.\]

Estimates A+B+C implies Global Regularity of Navier-Weber Fluid Equations According to BKM Criterion

The above estimates establish the regularity of incompressible Navier-Weber equations. These arguments are probably well-known to the experts.
